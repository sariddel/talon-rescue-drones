§ Executive Summary

Mission. A world where no rescue ends at the horizon, and no survivor waits beyond reach. Northreach Systems saves lives at sea — and extends what a warship can see — by delivering rapidly deployable drone reconnaissance from a vessel's own launch tubes.

The problem. A ship can only search the water it can physically reach. When a vessel goes down 70 nautical miles offshore, the nearest Coast Guard cutter is three to four hours away at 20 knots, and the search area grows with every minute of drift. A person in near-freezing water does not have hours. The same horizon that limits a rescue limits a destroyer's warning time against a surface or sub-surface contact.

The solution. TALON is a sealed, tube-launched drone cartridge that lives on a deck or shore rack and puts an autonomous search aircraft over the water in four minutes — streaming thermal and electro-optical video, flying an optimal search pattern, and dropping a GPS marker on a contact. One operator manages a swarm from the existing bridge console.

Why we win. A swarm of attritable drones costs a fraction of a vessel sortie — even if some are never recovered — and starts the search before anything else is on scene. Tube-launch from existing infrastructure, an automated search-theory planner, and a hardware-plus-sustainment model give us a defensible, recurring position with government and defence customers.

The ask. Northreach is raising a $6M seed round to complete development and maritime certification, field two Coast Guard pilots and one naval ISR evaluation, and reach a first program of record — the inflection to cash-flow-positive operations in Year 3.

I. Opportunity & Market Analysis

The Bering Sea is the most dangerous workplace in North America. The U.S. Coast Guard's 17th District patrols roughly 3.5 million square kilometres of remote, storm-prone ocean, and the nearest cutter to most active fishing grounds sits 60–90 nautical miles away. Between the distress call and the first responder on scene, the only thing that grows is the search area — wind and current push survivors further from their last-known position every minute. This gap between distress and arrival is where people die, and it is the gap TALON closes.

Customer segments

  • Coast Guard & national SAR authorities — the anchor customer. Mandated to respond, range-limited by hull speed, and acutely cost-sensitive to vessel and aviation hours.
  • Naval surface forces (ISR / awareness) — the same launch hardware, repurposed to push a warship's detection bubble over the horizon and hold an early track on surface and small-craft contacts without revealing the ship.
  • Offshore operators & fisheries — oil & gas platforms, large fishing fleets, and cruise operators with man-overboard and duty-of-care exposure far from shore-based rescue.

Market size

We frame the opportunity top-down then bottom-up to avoid the "we'll get 1% of a huge market" gap the framework warns against.

  • TAM — maritime ISR and unmanned maritime systems is a multi-billion-dollar and fast-growing global category spanning defence, coast guard, and commercial users.
  • SAM — tube-launched / rapidly-deployable small UAS for North American and allied coast guard and naval surface fleets: on the order of $300–600M annually across platforms and sustainment.
  • SOM (5-year) — a beachhead of a Coast Guard program of record plus early naval ISR adoption: a realistic $60–90M cumulative, built bottom-up from cutter counts, drones-per-cutter, and a recurring sustainment attach rate.
Market context

Three tailwinds favour entry now: proven attritable tube-launched airframes in defence service, plummeting thermal-imager cost, and a doctrinal shift toward distributed, low-cost, expendable sensing. The category is moving from "exquisite and few" to "cheap and many" — exactly where TALON sits.

II. The Solution & Concept

TALON is a search-and-rescue drone built around a cylindrical cartridge launch system for fast deployment and a gimballed thermal imager for human detection. It is compact enough that many units deploy simultaneously, and rugged enough to be treated as attritable — the system is designed so that losing a drone at sea is a cost line, not a mission failure.

A day in the life — before and after

Before: a Mayday lands; the cutter is hours out; the watch plots a last-known position and waits, knowing the drift circle is widening faster than the hull can close it. After: the watch fires a spread of tubes; within four minutes drones are climbing toward the datum; within the hour, thermal video has fanned across the drift circle, a survivor is marked with a GPS beacon, and the inbound helicopter is handed a fix instead of a search box.

Business model (seven elements, Table 3.8)

  • 1 · Customer selection. Primary customer is the Coast Guard and maritime safety officers, where time is critical and coverage-per-dollar is the buying criterion; naval ISR is the adjacent, higher-margin segment.
  • 2 · Value proposition. Rapid, compact, cartridge-based deployment with thermal live-streamed video that finds heat on cold water — improving survival rates and extending awareness range.
  • 3 · Differentiation & control. A persistent operator link, an automated search-theory planner, and patent protection on the launch-and-sensor integration; continuous innovation sustains the advantage and the cash flow.
  • 4 · Scope of product & activities. An integrated storage-and-launch system plus airframes, designed and assembled in-house, sold with the operator console.
  • 5 · Organizational design. R&D, engineering, manufacturing support, and quality assurance — with sustained field support as a first-class function, not an afterthought.
  • 6 · Value capture for profit. Hardware margin plus a recurring software, training, and sustainment contract; building from spare parts rather than pre-made assemblies protects the margin.
  • 7 · Value for talent. A cross-disciplinary team that wants to save lives, offered stable and meaningful work, real ownership of hard problems, and a healthy environment.

Uniqueness & defensibility

The defensible core is not any single sensor — it is the integration: a sealed launch cartridge that fields from existing tubes, a swarm autonomy and search-planning stack that turns coverage math into flight paths, and the dual-use bridge that lets one platform serve both a humanitarian search and a naval picture. Patents protect the launch/sensor integration; the data and sustainment relationship deepen switching costs over time.

III. Marketing & Sales

Government and defence selling is relationship- and proof-driven, with long cycles and high trust requirements. Our customer-development strategy runs in lock-step with product development: each milestone is demonstrated to a design-partner customer who shapes the next.

  • Channel. Direct to government for the anchor accounts, with prime-contractor partnerships to access programs of record and naval integration; indirect resellers for commercial offshore and fisheries.
  • Decision makers & influencers. The buyer is a program office and acquisition authority; the influencers are the operators — the cutter crews and watchstanders whose endorsement after a live demo carries disproportionate weight.
  • Best mediums. Live at-sea demonstrations, SAR and naval exercises, and defence/maritime trade events — not advertising. Seeing a tube fire and a survivor get marked is the marketing.
  • Sales cycle. Long — 12 to 36 months from first contact to a program — which is why we lead with low-commitment paid pilots that generate revenue and reference customers while the larger procurement matures.
  • Leverageable partnerships. Coast Guard innovation units, naval warfare centres, and established primes for integration and contract vehicles.

Development drives the amount and timing of cash, so the plan is staged to retire the biggest risks first and to put a working system in front of a customer early.

Value chain

In-house design and final assembly from a qualified component supply chain (airframes, thermal/EO modules, autopilot, launch cartridge), with a deliberately attritable bill of materials. Delivery is the integrated launch system, the airframes, and the operator console; revenue continues through munition-like cartridge resupply, software updates, and sustainment.

Intellectual property & regulatory

Patents on the launch/sensor integration and trade secrets in the autonomy and search-planning stack form the defendable moat. The principal external hurdle is regulatory: maritime airworthiness, beyond-visual-line-of-sight authorization, and — for the naval variant and any export — defence export controls. We treat certification as a Year-1/Year-2 milestone with budget and headcount assigned, not as a late surprise.

VI. Risks

A new venture faces four categories of risk; each is named here with how we manage it over the next year or two (p. 134).

Technology / product

Launch, recovery & detection in sea states

Tube-launch reliability and thermal detection degrade in rough seas. Mitigated by early, repeated sea trials and an attritable design that tolerates losses without mission failure.

Market / competition

Primes and other UAS entrants

Large defence primes could enter or acquire. Mitigated by a SAR beachhead they under-serve, the dual-use bridge, switching costs in sustainment, and IP on the integration.

Management / team

Execution & domain gaps

Government selling and certification need experience the founders lack. Mitigated by targeted senior hires and an operator-heavy advisory board.

Financial

Long cycles vs. burn

Procurement is slow relative to cash burn. Mitigated by paid pilots that generate early revenue and references, and staged financing tied to milestones.

Competitive response

Substitutes are vessels, crewed aircraft, and satellite cueing — all slower or coarser to a fresh datum. Against a fast-follower, our defence is to be the system operators already trust, with a certified, fielded, sustainment-backed product and the data advantage that comes from being first in the water.

VII. Financial Plan & Investment Offering

Northreach is raising a $6M seed round to fund development, certification, and the first pilots through to a program of record — the point at which the business turns cash-flow-positive. Financing is staged: this round retires technical and certification risk; a later Series A funds the scale-up against signed programs.

Key assumptions

  • Per-cutter fielding of a launch system plus a spread of drones, with a recurring cartridge-resupply and sustainment attach.
  • Pilots convert to programs at the rate strong operator references and a certified system support.
  • Largest cost buckets early are engineering and certification trials; later, manufacturing and field support.
  • Procurement is non-cyclical but slow; staged financing absorbs the timing gap between bookings and cash.
Analogous companies

The model rhymes with attritable tube-launched systems already in defence service (e.g. AeroVironment's tube-launched loitering munitions, Anduril's Altius family) and with maritime-autonomy ISR ventures (e.g. Saildrone). These validate both the tube-launch form factor and the hardware-plus-sustainment, government-anchored revenue model TALON pursues.
